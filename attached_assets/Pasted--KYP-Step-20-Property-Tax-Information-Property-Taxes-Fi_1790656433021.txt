## 1. What is wrong today

**The headline is half a bill.** `PROPERTY TAX BILL` leads with **$38,352.40**, which is the 2025
*first installment*. The last complete year was **$69,731.60**. An underwriter skimming the section
takes away a number that is 55% of the real one.

**And 55% is exactly the problem.** Cook County fixes the first installment at **55% of the prior
year's total bill**, by statute:

```
38,352.40 ÷ 69,731.60 = 0.5500      (2025 1st ÷ 2024 total)
39,690.79 ÷ 72,165.07 = 0.5500      (2024 1st ÷ 2023 total)
```

So the **"−3.4% vs prior 1st"** chip on the 2025 row is not a 2025 movement. Both installments are
0.55 × their prior-year totals, so the ratio between them *is* the 2023→2024 change — which is also
−3.4%. **The report shows a two-year-old movement under a current-year heading**, and the takeaway
reads it as "no unusual movement in the tax bill."

**Twelve appeal cards, each ~6 lines, mostly identical.** Nine of the twelve render the same
assessor and Board of Review value with `NO CHANGE`. The genuinely interesting content — *why* each
failed — is buried in an italic line inside each card.

**Exemption eligibility shows two veteran exemptions**, both `NOT ON FILE`, on a tenanted commercial
building. Both require primary residence. Neither can ever apply.

**The takeaway box duplicates the trigger row.** Four bullets with jump links, restating what the
four subsections below say. The owner is removing it; its job collapses into the one-liner.

## 2. Structure

Cut the takeaway box entirely. Four numbered subheads, one three-block row.

| | Subhead | Context slot |
|---|---|---|
| — | *(section blocks)* | last full-year bill · effective rate · appeal record |
| .1 | `THE BILL` | `Cook County Treasurer · paid through {latest installment}` |
| .2 | `ASSESSED VALUE` | `Cook County Assessor · tax year {year}` |
| .3 | `EXEMPTIONS` | `Cook County Assessor · {n} on file` |
| .4 | `APPEAL HISTORY` | `Board of Review · {n} filings · {m} reductions` |

Numbers from `SectionNumberContext` — **never hardcode `05`**.

**Exemptions sits above appeals, deliberately.** Exemptions answer a forward question: is this bill
the unsubsidised number, and will it survive the sale? Cook County exemptions do not transfer to a
buyer, so a seller's exemption means a step-up the year after closing. Appeals answer a backward
question about a previous owner's behaviour. The short consequential block goes before the long
archival one.

> ⚠️ **If an EAV walk is ever added** — $321,000 → equalizer → EAV → minus exemptions → × rate →
> billed — this order must flip back. Appeals act on the assessed value; exemptions act later on the
> EAV. That walk would be a strong addition to this section, and it is the one change that reverses §2.

⚠️ **Do not give any of the four a row of its own.** They are facets of one subject: what this parcel
costs in tax and why. One authority chain — Treasurer, Assessor, Board of Review — one row, four
subheads.

Add `propertyTax` to `ACC_DEFAULT_ORDER` and lift **the whole block, subheads and all**, out of the

**Suggested position: directly after `ownership`.** Who owns it, then what they pay on it. But this

## 4. Section blocks

| # | Class | `.bv` | `.bl` | `.bd` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ind` | `{lastFullYearTotal}` | `Last full-year bill` | which tax year, both installments, and that the current year is part-billed |
| 2 | `dark` | `{effectiveRate}` + `<u>%</u>` | `Of assessed market value` | the two operands, and that the buyer's own price will move it |
| 3 | `ind` | `{won}` + `<u>of {filed}</u>` | `Appeals won` | when, how much, and under which ownership |

**Block 1 must be the last *complete* year, not the current part-billed one.** When the second
installment posts, it becomes the current year automatically.

**Block 2 is new** — annual tax ÷ the assessor's implied market value. Both operands are already on
the page; nothing is fetched. For this parcel, $69,732 ÷ $3,210,000 = **2.17%**. It is the number
that ports straight into an underwriting model and the section does not currently produce it.

**Block 3 is a record, not a count.** `12` alone says the owner disputes their assessment. `1 of 12`
says the assessor's number has been tested eleven times and held — which is what a buyer wondering
"can I get this down?" actually needs. Go further in `.bd`: the single win was **2014**, under the
prior owner (Vassilopoulos / Moran). The current owner (West Bryn Mawr Owner LLC, via Bass and
Field) is **0 for 8**. That distinction is in the data and is invisible today.

All three blocks are indigo or black. **No verdict colour in this row** — a tax bill is a fact, not
a grade. Paid status rides on the accordion badge instead.

## 5. `.1` The bill

One `.kyp-dtab`: tax year · installments · billed · change · status.

**Delete the change figure for any part-billed year.** There is no year-over-year change until both
installments post. Render `—`.

**Show the change only between complete years.** 2024 vs 2023 = −3.4%.

One `.kyp-note` beneath, and it is the most important sentence in the section:

> **The first installment is not a signal.** Cook County sets it at **55% of the prior year's total
> bill**, by statute — {amount} is exactly 0.55 × the {prior} total. It carries no information about
> the {year} assessment or rate. The only real year-over-year change here is **{y1} to {y2}, {pct}**.

⚠️ **Assert the 0.55 relationship only after computing it.** If a future bill does not come out at
exactly 0.55 × prior total — a certificate of error, a prior-year adjustment, a divided parcel —
the sentence is false. Compute the ratio; if it is not 0.550, drop the note and show the figure plain.

Keep the Treasurer deep link.

## 6. `.2` Assessed value

Six facts in `.kyp-recgrid.inline`: assessed total (indigo cell) · assessment level · implied market
value · land · building · **land share**.

Land share is new and computed — $140,625 ÷ $321,000 = 44%. It is what makes the trend chart legible.

⚠️ **Verify the assessment level against the property class.** The section states *assessed at 10%
of market*. That is the Cook County **residential** level; commercial is **25%**. This is a B3-5
parcel. If the class is wrong the implied market value is out by 2.5× — and block 2's effective rate
with it. Do not change the figure on my say-so; confirm it against the class code and fix the source
if it is wrong.

### 6.1 The chart

**Keep the existing colours.** Blue total assessed, orange building, green dashed land. These are
chart series, not verdict chips, and the palette rule does not reach them — do not "correct" orange
to something in the verdict scale.

Two changes only: **label the two events** the data actually shows — the 2014 appeal win and the 2022
reassessment step — with dashed vertical rules, and **thin the x-axis** to every third year so the
labels stop crowding.

One `.kyp-note` on what the shape means: land has risen from $75,937 to $140,625 since 2013 while the
building assessment fell and recovered — the assessor has reweighted toward the ground. Cook County
reassesses on a three-year cycle, so the steps matter more than the flat stretches.

## 7. `.3` Exemptions and `.4` Appeals

### 7.1 Exemptions

Three facts in `.kyp-recgrid.inline`: on file · history for this PIN · **effect on the bill**.

**Cut both eligibility cards.** Every Cook County exemption — homeowner, senior, senior freeze,
veteran — requires the parcel to be the claimant's **primary residence**. None can attach to a
tenanted building, so listing two of them as `NOT ON FILE` states a structural impossibility as
though it were a gap. One note says so, and keeps the cookcountyassessor.com pointer for the case
where occupancy changes.

⚠️ **Only cut them where the property cannot qualify.** On an owner-occupied parcel the eligibility
list is genuinely useful and must still render. The filter is occupancy, not property tax class.

### 7.2 Appeals

Twelve cards become one `.kyp-dtab`: tax year · assessor · Board of Review · outcome · filed by.
**The reason moves to a dimmed second line under the year** — it is the interesting part and it
survives at full detail.

Reading the reasons as a column surfaces something the cards hide: **three of the eleven failures are
procedural, not valuational** — no evidence filed, Board of Review rules not met, nothing beyond the
complaint form. That is a pattern about how this owner appeals.

`DECREASE` renders green; `NO CHANGE` renders neutral. Sort newest first.

**Who filed them** keeps its win-loss records and its caveat, compressed to three rows. The caveat is
required: these count filings **on this parcel only**, not a representative's overall record.

## 9. Branches I could not see

- **Delinquent or unpaid.** Every row here reads `PAID`. What renders on an unpaid balance, a sold
  tax lien, or a payment plan? That is the single most consequential state this section has and I
  have never seen it. It needs a red badge and a place in the one-liner.
- **A part-billed year with no prior full year** — a new PIN, a divided parcel. Block 1 has no value.
- **Exemptions present.** The whole of §6.1 inverts: the eligibility cards must render, and the
  one-liner should say what the exemption is worth and that it does not transfer.
- **No appeal history.** `.4` should not render an empty table; follow the Step 9c `not_found`
  pattern.
- **Assessment history shorter than the chart window** — a recently created PIN.
- **A certificate of error or PTAB decision** changing a prior year after the fact. Two of the cards
  mention PTAB; nothing shows its outcome.
