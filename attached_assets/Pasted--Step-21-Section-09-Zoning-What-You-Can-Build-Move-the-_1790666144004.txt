## 1. 🔴 There are three verdicts, not two

`RunDetail.tsx:4961` branches on `effectiveCompatibility.permission`:

| value | line | renders as |
|---|---|---|
| `permitted` | 4985 | `cyp-verdict ok` — green |
| `special_use` | 5013 | `cyp-verdict **bad**` — red |
| *(else, rezoning)* | 5029 | `cyp-verdict **bad**` — red |

**Special Use is painted as a denial.** Its own copy, on line 5019, reads:

> *"That's a defined, winnable path, not a denial."*

…inside a card whose background is `#fbeeee` with a `#ecc9c4` border and a `#c0392b` icon — the
identical treatment given to "not permitted, rezoning required". The sentence argues with its own
styling, and the two genuinely different outcomes are indistinguishable at a glance.

This is the substantive fix in this step. A Special Use is a ZBA hearing — months, a defined
standard, no parcel rezoning. A Map Amendment is City Council — far longer, politically contingent.
They are not the same answer.

**They split across the scale — they do not both go orange, and they do not both stay red:**

| | verdict | chip | headline | block 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `permitted` | **green** | `ALLOWED BY RIGHT` | *No zoning relief needed* | green · `By right` |
| `special_use` | **orange** | `SPECIAL USE REQUIRED` | *A defined path, not a denial* | orange · `Special use` · Zoning Board of Appeals |
| rezoning | **red** | `NOT PERMITTED AS-OF-RIGHT` | *A map amendment, not a variance* | red · `Rezoning` · City Council, not the ZBA |

Red stays where it is today for the use that is genuinely not compliant — a Gas Station is not a
listed use in B1-3, and rezoning the parcel is a different order of undertaking. What changes is that
**Special Use stops borrowing that red.** The ordinance contemplates the use in the district; it just
wants a hearing. The section's own Ward 1 table makes the case: 97 cases, **zero denials**. That is
not a `bad` verdict, and the copy on line 5019 already says so.

This uses the full scale — green / orange / red across three genuinely different answers — rather
than collapsing two of them into one colour, which is what happens today.
