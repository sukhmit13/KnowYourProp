The honest FAQ.

What a report can tell you, what it can't, and where our numbers come from. No overpromising — that's the whole point of the product.

Before you make an offer
Why get a report before I make an offer?
Once you're under contract, you've usually put down earnest money (often 1–3% of the price — $5,000 to $50,000+) and you're paying an attorney ($1,500–$3,000) to review the deal on a tight clock. Anything you find at that point, you find with your money and your leverage already committed. A report puts the full picture in front of you first — so you can walk, renegotiate, or move ahead knowing exactly what you're buying.
Can I actually use this to negotiate?
It's one of the most common ways people use it. If the record shows a lien, an open violation, or a use that needs a costly zoning approval, that's a documented reason to adjust your offer — for example, "there's a recorded lien on this property, so I'm reflecting that in my number." A documented fact is much harder to wave away than a hunch.
What if I find something the seller didn't disclose?
That's the report doing its job. Found before you offer, an undisclosed lien or violation is just information — you can pass, price it in, or ask the seller to clear it as a condition of the deal. Found after you're under contract, the same issue becomes a fight on a deadline. Earlier is always cheaper.
What's in a report
What does a report actually cover?
The public record on a Chicago property, pulled into one place: ownership and sale history, recorded liens and mortgages, building-code violations and permit history, property-tax status and trajectory, zoning and what it allows, location-based incentive zones, and neighborhood market and demographic data. Instead of a dozen county and city systems that don't talk to each other, one plain-language brief.
Does the report change based on what I want to do?
Yes — that's the point of the few questions we ask up front. Tell us you're opening a daycare and the report leans into the zoning, special-use, and childcare-demand picture; tell us you're a residential buyer and it focuses there. Same address, a different report, built around your plan. Prefer to skip the questions? You'll still get the full data report.
How current is the data?
We pull from public sources daily, so most data is current within 24–48 hours. A few sources lag by nature — recorded sales and permit filings can trail 30–90 days depending on how quickly the county and city post them. When something is time-sensitive, the report flags it.
What areas do you cover?
All of Chicago and Cook County, Illinois today. It's one of the most layered property markets in the country — dense zoning, TIF districts, landmark rules, dozens of overlapping incentive programs — which is exactly why we started here. More markets are on the way.
Accuracy & limits
How accurate is the lien search?
We search both the property PIN and the owner's name against the Cook County Recorder, which gets us 75%+ coverage — and we'd rather tell you that plainly than claim 100%. Some liens are recorded in other venues or are still pending recording when you look. Treat the report as your early-warning system, and always confirm with a title company before closing.
Is this a replacement for a title company?
No, and we won't pretend otherwise. We're pre-purchase due diligence — the picture you want before you commit money. You'll still need a title company at closing for title insurance and final lien clearance. Think of us as the step that comes before you ever get there.
Can you tell me I'm "definitely eligible" for a tax incentive?
We tell you which incentive zones a property falls inside — Opportunity Zones, TOD areas, and the like — because that's a matter of public record. Whether you actually qualify depends on your project, your structure, and program rules, so final eligibility is a conversation for a tax advisor. We'll show you where to look; we won't guess for you.
What if the report has incorrect information?
Public records aren't perfect — a county system can carry a typo, a stale entry, or a lag before an update posts. We'd much rather you flag it than quietly lose trust in the whole report. If something looks wrong, email support@knowyourprop.com with the address and what you're seeing, and we'll check it against the source and correct it. For anything time-sensitive, the report also points you to the original source so you can verify before you act.
