Why it pays to look first

What's hiding in the public record.

A listing won't show these. A home inspection won't catch them. The examples below are illustrative — but the problems, and what they cost, are exactly what a report is built to surface before you're committed.

These scenarios are illustrative examples of what the public record can reveal — not specific customer results. The costs shown are typical Chicago ranges (a home inspection runs ~$400–600; earnest money is usually 1–3% of the purchase price; Illinois attorney-review windows are often about five days).
Scenario 01Under contract · ~5-day window

Violations, an orange tag, and no room to build

  • You're under contract on a single-family home. The listing looked clean.
The record reveals
  • Open building-code violations, plus an orange historic rating — no facade changes without city approval.
  • A maxed-out FAR — the addition you were planning would need a costly zoning variance.
Why it matters: a Chicago home inspection runs ~$400–600 and flags none of this. Catch it during attorney review and you can walk with your earnest money — or renegotiate — before you've spent a dollar on the inspection.
Scenario 02Before the offer

A lien that never appears on the listing

  • You've found a strong investment property and you're about to make an offer.
The record reveals
  • A mechanics lien from unpaid contractor work — the kind that can run into the tens of thousands and clouds the title until it's cleared.
Why it matters: surfacing it before you offer keeps your earnest money (1–3% of the price) and your attorney fees off the table. After you're under contract, it becomes a fight.
Scenario 03Wrong site for the use

“Perfect for a daycare” — until it isn't

  • A storefront looks ideal to open a daycare center.
The record reveals
  • The use needs a Special Use Permit — a months-long Zoning Board process and thousands in fees.
Why it matters: knowing up front, you keep looking and find a site zoned for daycare as-of-right — no hearing, no delay. That use-based check is exactly what the report runs when you tell it your plan.
Scenario 04Discovered too late

A lien on the owner, not the property

  • You're under contract on a condo.
The record reveals
  • A judgment lien against the owner that must be cleared before they can legally sell.
Why it matters: discovered after you're committed, it can freeze the deal for months — and the earnest money and the opportunity can both be lost. Seen before you offer, you simply move on to the next one.

Catch it before you offer — not at the closing table.

The public record holds the answer the whole time. A report just puts it in front of you before the money's committed. At $29, it's the cheapest insurance you can buy on a six-figure decision.
