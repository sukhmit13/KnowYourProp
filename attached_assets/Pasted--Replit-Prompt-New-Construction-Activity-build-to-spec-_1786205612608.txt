## LOGIC (compute in code — NOT an LLM)

### 1. Volume-proportional bars (the core of the chart)
- `maxTotal` = the largest single-year total across shown years.
- Each bar's outer width `{BAR_PCT}` = `round(yearTotal / maxTotal * 100, 2)`. This makes bar length = **volume** — the whole reason the chart exists. Do NOT normalize every bar to 100%.
- Inside each bar, the three `<i>` segments carry `style="flex:{count}"` for SF / MF / Commercial **that year** — so segment widths are that year's type mix, and the bar's total length is that year's volume. (`flex:N` with `display:block` height:100% — inline spans would collapse; they must be block.)
- 2px gap between segments (`.ybbar{gap:2px}`), 5px rounded ends only on the outer corners (already in CSS).

### 2. Full years vs. YTD (partial current year)
- Derive from the **actual max permit date** in the dataset, not a hardcoded year. The latest calendar year is YTD **iff** the dataset's max date is before Dec 31 of that year.
- YTD year: add the `<span class="yt">YTD</span>` sublabel under the year, and render the `{PACE_CHIP}`:
  `<span class="ybpace">Through {MONTH} · ~{PACE}/yr pace</span>`
- Full years: no sublabel, no pace chip.
- **Never mix partial and full in the trend claim without saying so** (see LOGIC 3).

### 3. Pace + trend (the integrity fix)
- `{PACE}` = `round(ytdCount / fractionOfYearElapsed)`, where `fractionOfYearElapsed = dayOfYear(maxDate) / 365`. Straight-line projection.
- **Always caveat the pace as a straight-line projection** and note peak-season skew (Chicago building peaks spring–summer, so a Jan–Aug window over-projects). Copy the reference trend text.
- Trend word (chip): compute over **full years only** (ignore the YTD year for the label). Flat/no-clear-direction → "Stable"; consistent multi-year rise → "Rising"; consistent fall → "Cooling". Don't call it a direction the full years don't support.

### 4. Skew flag on averages
- Flag a type's average as `· skewed` when its mean is dominated by outliers — e.g. mean ≫ median (ratio over ~2×), or a single permit is a large share of that type's total declared value. Commercial is the usual case (few permits, huge megaprojects). Compute from the permit values; don't hardcode.

### 5. Two DIFFERENT scopes — keep them separate (do not conflate)
The section mixes two scopes that are **not** subsets of each other:
- **Community-area, since {START_YEAR}** → drives the header count, the 3 stat cards, and the yearly bars. State = permits *issued* in the range.
- **1-mile radius, active now** → the `{ACTIVE_1MI}` number (132). State = permits *currently active*, radius around the parcel.
These have different denominators AND different states (issued-in-range vs. active-now). **Never imply one is a subset of the other**, never add them, never say "of the 220, 132 are…". The takeaway leads with `{ACTIVE_1MI}` because it's the most decision-relevant read (current + proximate to the parcel); the community-area history is the backdrop that earns the stability claim. Keep them as two independently-bound values.
- The scope toggle (By Community Area / By ZIP / Nearby 1 mi) re-scopes the stat cards + bars; the active-1mi number is its own datum shown regardless.

### 6. Methodology note
- Source line states the exclusions the backend applies (garages, stages & other accessory structures) and the exact date range, and flags the current year as YTD. Keep this — it's a data-quality signal that builds trust.
