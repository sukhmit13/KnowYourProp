🔴 **Scope:** `RunDetail.tsx` **9948–10119** — the whole section, **172 lines, read at 100%.**
Ships after Step 13b. Replaces the earlier Step 15 document, which was written from ~23% of the

---

## 0. The thing to fix first — the section overstates what the rating gives you

The takeaway and `What This Means` both say a survey rating **may qualify** the property for
**Class L**, **Federal Historic 20%** and **Illinois Historic 25%**. Checked against the programme
rules, none of the three is available on a survey rating alone:

| Credit | Actual requirement | Survey rating enough? |
|---|---|---|
| Class L | **Municipal landmark designation** (individual, or contributing in a designated district) | **No** |
| Federal Historic 20% | **National Register listing** *and* income-producing use — NPS excludes an owner's private residence | **No** |
| Illinois Historic 25% | **National Register listing** | **No** |

A CHRS colour code is the City's record that a building is significant. **Designation is a separate
act of City Council.** The current copy hedges with "may qualify", but a reader underwriting a rehab
will price those credits in, and they are not there.

**The one thing that *is* automatic** is the opposite: an Orange- or Red-coded building has its
demolition permit **held 90 days** under Chicago Municipal Code **§14A-4-407.6** — no hearing, no
discretion, nothing to apply for. Today that is buried in a bullet as "Demolition permits require
Commission on Chicago Landmarks review", which understates it by naming a review rather than a
duration.

**Invert the emphasis.** What binds is certain and immediate. What pays is conditional and distant.

Equally: the section currently implies exterior alterations face restrictions. On a survey-rated,
undesignated building they do not. Say so — a false constraint costs a reader as much as a false
benefit.

## 1. Structure

No subheads. **One fact with two consequences does not need dividing.**

1. `.kyp-blocks hero` — three **indigo** blocks (`.ind`; `.ind-deep` on a Red-rated parcel)
2. `.kyp-chrstier` — the survey's own four-tier scale, this property marked
3. `.kyp-says lg` — **What the rating triggers** | **What it does not unlock**
4. `.kyp-caveat` — a survey rating is not a designation
5. `.kyp-src`

### 1.1 Hero blocks

| # | `.bv` | `.bl` | `.bbreak` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the rating word | `Survey rating — {n}nd of 4 tiers` | `Means` the tier's significance |
| 2 | `90 days` | `Demolition permit held — automatic on this rating` | `Rule` Municipal Code §14A-4-407.6 |
| 3 | era | `Era recorded · {n} tagged parcel(s) on site` | `Survey only` not a designated landmark |

**The blocks stay indigo — never filled with the survey colour.** A survey code is a state, not a
verdict. Only the *rating word* takes the survey hue, at its exact brand value, on the deep indigo
substrate. See §2.1 for why the block darkens rather than the word lightening.

**Block 2 renders only for Orange and Red.** Yellow and Green carry no hold; for those the block is
dropped and the row falls back to `kyp-blocks hero two`. Never render a 90-day hold on a code that
does not trigger one.

**Block 3 defers to the county** on the exact year — CHRS records a decade, the assessor records a
year, and the county is the baseline.

### 1.2 The four-tier legend — `.kyp-chrstier`

Four cells: Red / Orange / Yellow / Green, each a labelled swatch plus one line of what the tier
means. The property's own tier gets `.on` and `· this property`.

This is the **only** place the survey colours appear at any size, and every swatch is labelled. That
is what lets the palette carry meaning at all (§2). It also answers the question the rating alone
raises — *is Orange good or bad?* — by showing the scale rather than asserting a position on it.

### 1.3 The two columns

**Left — `WHAT THE RATING TRIGGERS`.** Only what is in force now.

**Right — `WHAT IT DOES NOT UNLOCK`.** Not "may unlock". Each credit with a `.req` line naming the
requirement the parcel fails. Where a parcel *is* designated, `.req` becomes `.req.met` and names
the designation that qualifies it.

**The verdict itself lives in `07 · Location Incentives`,** which already runs the eligibility
check. This section states the requirement and points there — one direction, no duplicated
reconciliation. Location Incentives' own takeaway should read
`Class L, Federal 20%, Illinois 25% — all not applicable without designation.`

## 2. CHRS colours — from your brand palette, and never load-bearing alone

The survey colours now come from the tokens already in `index.css`, three of them annotated as the
logo palette:

| Tier | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Red | `#d13b26` | `--ref-red` / `--bad` — *"logo red"* |
| Orange | `#ff6b35` | `--color-orange` |
| Yellow | `#f3b31f` | `--ref-yellow` / `--caution` — *"logo gold"* |
| Green | `#2fb457` | `--ref-green` `#2f7d3f` raised for brightness |

Only green is not an exact brand token — `--ref-green` is the muted one, so it is lifted in the same
hue family. Everything else is the brand value, unchanged.

**This also happens to fix a real accessibility defect.** The shipped survey palette fails badly:

| | worst pair, normal | worst pair, deuteranomaly | worst pair, protanomaly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped today | ΔE 16.2 | **ΔE 1.0 — Orange and Yellow are the same swatch** | ΔE 4.3 |
| Brand palette | ΔE 16.3 | **ΔE 11.2** | **ΔE 11.6** |

Deuteranomaly affects roughly 6% of men. At ΔE 1.0 an Orange rating and a Yellow rating are
indistinguishable; at 11.2 they are clearly different. The brighter values earn this — saturation is
what carries separation once hue collapses.

It also pulls the survey orange clear of the verdict palette: **CHRS orange vs verdict orange goes
from ΔE 2.7 to 9.7**, so the two oranges stop reading as the same signal.

Replace `getChrsColor` with these four values, keeping its existing comment.

The survey colours appear **only** on `.kyp-chrsw` swatches, in the `.kyp-chrstier` legend, and as
the hero block's display word — never as a fill behind body text, never on a chip alone.

### 2.1 The rating word uses the exact brand value — never a lightened tint

`.bv.chrs-{colour}` carries the **unmodified** brand hex, the same value as the tag and the swatch.
An earlier draft lightened it to `#ffd0bf` to chase contrast; that greyed the brand colour out and
read as faded. **Do not lighten the rating word.**

The blocks stay `--kyp-indigoL` — the same indigo as every other hero row in the report. On it:

| tier | luminance contrast | chromatic separation |
|---|---|---|
| orange `#ff6b35` | 2.25:1 | ΔE 68 |
| gold `#f3b31f` | 3.43:1 | ΔE 75 |
| green `#2fb457` | 2.37:1 | ΔE 60 |
| **red `#d13b26`** | **1.33:1** | ΔE 63 |

Orange, gold and green read clearly — at 46px with ΔE 60+ of chromatic separation, and with the
rating also stated in the tag, the `.bl` label, the tier legend and the takeaway sentence, the word
is reinforcement rather than the sole carrier.

**Red is the exception.** At 1.33:1 a dark red on mid blue goes muddy, and it looks it. For a
**Red-rated property only**, the whole hero row uses `.kyp-block.ind-deep` (`#192261`), where red
reaches 3.03:1. A property has exactly one tier, so no reader ever sees the two block shades side by
side.

⚠️ This is a deliberate exception to a 3:1 large-text target on three tiers, taken because the
alternative — a tinted brand colour, or a dark row on every property — is worse. If it should hold
the line instead, the fix is `.ind-deep` on all four tiers, and the row goes navy everywhere.

⚠️ **One residual:** CHRS red `#d13b26` sits ΔE 4.5 from verdict red `#c0392b`. They are close, but
CHRS red only ever appears as a labelled swatch, and a Red rating means *National Register calibre* —
which is not a bad outcome. Worth knowing; not worth breaking the brand match over.

**The tag reads `Orange tag`, never `CHRS orange`.** The row badge itself is **indigo** — the badge
marks a state, and only the swatch inside it carries the survey hue.

## 3. Conditional promotion, not a permanent row

**~97% of Chicago parcels carry no survey rating.** So this is neither a permanent top-level section
nor a buried subsection.

**Promote it to its own accordion row only when the parcel is survey-rated, individually designated,
or inside a landmark district.** On every other property it does not exist as a row at all — the
fact becomes a single line inside `05 · Zoning & What You Can Build`:
*"No historic designation on record."*

That keeps a real constraint at full weight where it applies and off the screen entirely where it
does not. An empty "Not a Landmark" row on 97% of reports is noise.

**Development Potential also carries the hold.** Where it states what can be built, one line —
*"A demolition permit here is held 90 days (§14A-4-407.6)"* — linking back. That is the only fact
allowed in two places, because it changes the answer in both.

### 4.3 `isNearbyMatch` is currently a `<small>` and should not be

When the surveyed address's house number differs from the subject's, today the section prints
`· nearest historic resource, adjacent to 522` in small grey text **inside** the address field —
below three blocks and a takeaway that have already asserted the building is historic.

**It means the rating may describe the building next door.** Render it as a `.kyp-caveat` **above**
the hero blocks, same as `foundOnCoParcel`. Standing rule: where figures describe something other
than this address, say so before them.

## 5. Empty and error states

`isRelevant === false` — **the section does not render.** Per §3 the row is not created at all, and
`05 · Zoning & What You Can Build` carries the one-line statement instead.

`landmarkData` null — keep `Unable to check landmark status` verbatim, as `.kyp-empty`. An unread
record is not an absent one.
