## The problem, stated once

§22 explains the same caveat in four places before a reader reaches a number.

| Where | Says |
|---|---|
| Section kicker (Scan) | "Readable counts only; some permitted addresses have unknown units" |
| Card `.bd` — `~192` | "across 24 permits issued within 18 months · from the permit record · unit count estimated from permit descriptions · units unknown at 16 permitted addresses; only readable counts shown" |
| `pipefoot` paragraph | 95 words, four sentences, one of them bolded |
| `.kyp-src` footer | the counting rule again |

The locked rule is **one note per block, at the end**. §22 has four layers.

The test that separates them: **is this a fact about the number, or a fact about how we got the
number?** A fact about the number belongs beside the number. A fact about how we got it belongs
in the footer, once.

`24 permits` is a fact about the number. `an issued permit within 18 months is a proxy, not
confirmation construction is underway` is a fact about how we got it. Today they sit in the same
sentence.

### The sentence that layout should be doing

> **The two figures are not added together**

This is the clearest symptom. Three identical cards sit in one row; two of their values must never
be summed; a bolded sentence underneath asks the reader not to. **If two numbers must never be
summed, do not sit them side by side in a row of three identical cards.** Label the stages and the
sentence is unnecessary.

### The 12-month change is slate, not green or orange

A `+125%` with an arrow is the single most likely thing in this step to get colored by reflex.
It must not be.

The locked rule: **a block takes a verdict color only when an outside authority has already
classified the value and set its polarity.** Nobody publishes a cut point for nearby permit
growth, and the polarity depends entirely on why the reader is here — a developer reads +125% as
momentum, an owner of competing units reads it as supply. Where polarity depends on intended use,
the block is **slate**. No arrow glyph either.
