The Takeaway
A fast-appreciating, renter-majority submarket with sharply rising incomes, a multilingual customer base, and an electorate that has voted directly on property-cost policy.
Incomes are climbing fast. Median household income rose 38.5% to $141,179 and per-capita income 58.9%, while poverty fell to 8.3% — a widening income base that underpins higher rents.ACS 5-yr
Renter-majority and tightening. 67.5% renter-occupied (owner share down 6.5 pts to 32.5%), vacancy compressed to 6.3%, gross rents up 35% to $2,216 — durable demand for a buy-to-lease thesis.
The entry basis has run hard. Median home value climbed 41.7% to $604,200 — the rent and income growth are real, but appreciation this fast argues for underwriting to today's price, not the trailing trend.Verify basis
A multilingual customer & tenant base. 31.5% speak a language other than English at home (Spanish 15.8%, Polish 4.5%; 8.8% limited-English). Under B3 retail zoning that's an operational signal — bilingual signage/staffing for a customer-facing use, and tenant-outreach reach for a landlord.ACS
Voters have weighed property-cost policy directly. The 2024 real-estate transfer-tax measure (Bring Chicago Home) failed; a $15 minimum wage passed — measures that bear on transaction and operating costs. Local turnout runs high.Elections
Transit-oriented, entertainment daytime economy. 38% commute by transit; a net bedroom community (−1,460 daytime) with local jobs concentrated in food service and arts — foot-traffic character for a corridor retail use.
