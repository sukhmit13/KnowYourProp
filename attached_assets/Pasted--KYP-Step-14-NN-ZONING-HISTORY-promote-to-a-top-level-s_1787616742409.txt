## 1. Promote it, and give it a takeaway

Zoning History becomes a **top-level accordion row**. It has a subject of its own — the recorded
legislative actions on this parcel — and it is the only section that answers "has anyone tried to
change what this parcel is allowed to be?"

`05 · Zoning & What You Can Build` answers *what is allowed now*. This section answers *how it got
that way, and who did the work*. They are different questions and both are load-bearing.

### The takeaway is a finding, not a description

Today the section subtitle reads *"Ordinances, rezonings, and ZBA cases for this address"* — the
same string on every parcel in Chicago. Replace it with a computed sentence:

| Condition | Takeaway |
|---|---|
| A Board order states the existing unit configuration | `Subdivided in {year} — the Board's order kept all {n} units, front and rear.` |
| Amendment names a **different** development address (§2.1) | `Split from {addr}'s zoning lot in {year} — {the parcel-specific caveat}.` |
| Rezoning, sale within 24 months before it, no permit since, **and** §2.1's four gates pass | `Rezoned {n} months after the {year} sale — and nothing built since.` |
| Rezoning, permits followed | `Rezoned to {district} in {year} — built out {year}.` |
| Rezoning, no sale correlation | `Rezoned to {district} in {year} — {n} years ago.` |
| ZBA grant on record | `{Approved\|Denied} at the Zoning Board — {case} in {year}.` |
| No actions at all | `No rezoning or ZBA case on record — {district} since the 2004 code rewrite.` |
| Still loading | render a skeleton, **not** a sentence |

Badge: `{n} actions · {district}` in indigo — count **cases**, not hearing entries (§5.0). **Indigo, not green or orange** — a zoning history
is a state, not a verdict. There is no good or bad number of rezonings.

**When there are no actions on record, render no badge and leave the row collapsed.** That is the
normal state for most Chicago parcels; it is not a finding and it should not occupy a screen. The
takeaway carries the whole answer.

## 2. The finding — and a trap to avoid on the way to it

The Councilmatic extraction now returns the application's stated purpose. For 522 N Claremont it
says: the amendment was filed **to develop 520 N Claremont with a new single-family home**; 520 and
522 **comprised one zoning lot**; the amendment lets that lot **be divided**; and the existing
**multi-unit building and detached coach house at 522 would remain**.

### 2.1 Do not compute "rezoned then never built" on this parcel

The obvious-looking finding — sold Dec 2013, rezoned Jul 2014, **zero permits since** — is wrong
here, and an earlier draft of this spec asserted it.

The development was proposed at **520**. A different address. Zero permits at 522 is precisely
what the filing anticipated: 522's buildings were to stay as they are. Rendering it as a stalled
project invents a problem out of the section's own success.

**Rule: a rezoning's outcome must be measured at the address the application names, not at the
report's subject address.** They are frequently different — that is the whole point of a
lot-division amendment.

Gate the correlation on all of:

1. The application's stated development address **matches this parcel**, or no development address
   was extracted at all.
2. A qualifying transfer falls within 24 months before the rezoning.
3. The permit count at **this** parcel since the rezoning is zero.
4. The transfer record is non-empty (Step 7b §3.3 — fail closed on zero transfers).

Where the development address is a **different** parcel, do not treat the permit count as evidence
of anything, and do not write a paragraph about it either. Hero block 3 carries it — see §3.

**No "Pattern" callout.** An earlier draft opened the section with a prose block narrating the
sale → rezoning → permits sequence. It is gone. The takeaway states the finding, the hero blocks
carry the facts, and the section does not also argue its case in a paragraph. If a correlation
cannot be said in the takeaway plus three blocks, it is not established well enough to assert.

### 2.2 The real finding is the rear unit — and the Board settled more of it than the application did

For a buyer of 522 the load-bearing fact is how many dwelling units legally stand here. Two records
speak to it, and they are **not** equal weight:

- The **2014 application** (applicant's words): a multi-unit building and a detached coach house
  "would remain." That is a statement of intent made to win an approval.
- **ZBA order 70-17-Z, Jun 16 2017** (the Board's words): *"The existing two-story, two dwelling
  unit front building and one dwelling unit rear building will remain."* The Board granted setback
  relief **on that basis**.

A Board order predicated on a configuration is materially stronger evidence than an applicant
describing one — but it is still not a certificate of occupancy, and the section must not upgrade
it into one. Cook County records `Two (primary bldg of 2) Apts`; the rear unit is not in that
figure. Three dwellings, two of them counted.

Takeaway: **`Subdivided in 2017 — the Board's order kept all three units, front and rear.`**

Both the subdivision and the unit count are verified facts about **this** parcel. The variation
package next door (§5.1, Matter 2) is context, not the headline — it concerns the other lot.

## 4. `WHAT CHANGED`, then `WHAT THE RECORD ESTABLISHES`

### 4.1 The district diff

With Councilmatic extraction the diff is real: **RS-3 + RT-4 → RT-4**. Render it as a two-column
`.kyp-xfer`.

The "before" value is doing more work than it looks. `RS-3 + RT-4` means the lot was
**split-zoned** — one zoning lot carrying two districts. Say that, because it is the reason the
amendment was needed at all: you cannot cleanly divide a zoning lot whose halves sit in different
districts. A reader who sees two codes and no explanation will assume a typo.

The "after" column keeps the standing note that RT-4 is **confirmed against the current zoning
map, not inferred from ordinance text.**

**When extraction fails** — and it still will, since it depends on a published PDF and a regex —
render `Not in the record` at full size in the before column rather than hedging in prose, and
say what would close the gap: the pre-amendment zoning map for the block, which a zoning attorney
can pull in an afternoon. An honest empty cell shows the reader exactly which half of the question
we answered.

### 4.2 `WHAT THE RECORD ESTABLISHES` — split what is found from what is still open

This is the new subsection the better extraction earns, and it is where the section becomes worth
more than the record it summarizes. Two columns — **Found in the record** and
**Still to confirm**:

| **Stated in the application** (indigo) | **What it does not establish** (amber) |
|---|---|
| The purpose was to develop 520 N Claremont with a new single-family home. | This is application context, **not a variance**. Nothing here grants relief from RT-4's bulk or density limits. |
| 520 and 522 comprised one zoning lot; the amendment allows it to be divided. | It is **not a blanket grandfathering approval**. Describing the coach house is not the same as legalizing it. |
| The existing multi-unit building and detached coach house at 522 would remain. | **Verify the legal unit count separately.** Cook County records two apartments in the primary building; the coach house is a third dwelling and its status is not settled by this document. |

### 4.3 Colour: the right column is slate, not amber

An earlier draft rendered "what it does not establish" in amber with amber bullets. **Wrong, and
against our own rule** — amber is not in the categorical palette, and the custom brown used
(`#a65f14`) was off-system entirely.

More importantly it was the wrong *claim*. Amber means **watch — this needs checking**, which
asserts a problem. We are not asserting one: we do not know the coach house is unlawful, and
Chicago's ADU history makes it genuinely ambiguous. This column is **context** — the boundary of
what one document proves. That is slate, on `--wash`, with slate bullets. No verdict colour on
either side of this panel.

The same applies to the ZBA lapse line: the closed-window fact renders in `--ink2` with the date
bolded, and the row's decision chip carries the only colour. A stray brown for "expired" was the
same mistake in a different place.

### 4.4 Bold the finding, not the setup

Each bullet leads with what happened and bolds that clause. The earlier draft bolded
*"comprised one zoning lot"* — the prior state, not the action — which buried the point in the
middle of a sentence and made the panel hard to scan.

Left column, each bullet opening with the operative fact: **The zoning lot was divided.** /
**520 was to be developed** / **522's buildings were to stay**. Right column parallel and short:
**Not a variance** / **Not a grandfathering approval** / **Not a unit count**.

Parallel openings let a reader take the whole panel in one pass. One bold phrase per bullet, at
the front.

⚠️ Render the left column as **the applicant's words**, labelled as such in the subhead. Never
restate an application's description in the product's own voice. An applicant's description is a
statement of intent made to win an approval — evidence of what was *proposed*, never a
determination of what is *lawful*.

## 5. `THE RECORD` — group by matter, one row per case, every field once

### 5.1 The full record duplicates cases — 8 entries for 4 cases

With the ZBA fetch fixed, 522 N Claremont returns **eight entries**:

| Date | Case | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 21, 2017 | 71-17-Z · 72-17-Z · 73-17-Z | Approved |
| Jun 16, 2017 | 70-17-Z | Approved |
| Jun 16, 2017 | 71-17-Z · 72-17-Z · 73-17-Z | **Continued** |
| Jul 30, 2014 | O2014-4194 | Passed |

Cases 71, 72 and 73 each render **twice** — once `Continued` on Jun 16, once `Approved` on Jul 21.
A continuance is not an action; it is the same case being held over to the next hearing. Rendering
it as a peer row makes a four-case docket look like eight, and puts a grey `Continued` chip
directly above a green `Approved` chip **for the same case number** — which reads as a contradiction.

**Fix: key rows by case number.** One row per case, carrying its **final disposition**. Earlier
hearing dates render as a hearing track on that row, not as rows of their own:

```
[VARIATION] [APPROVED]                                        Jul 21, 2017
Reduce minimum lot area per dwelling unit 3,000 → 2,884.37 sf
· Case 71-17-Z  continued from Jun 16
```

Where a case is **still** continued with no later disposition, that is its current status and it
renders as one row reading `Continued` — an open matter, indigo, not a verdict.

Keep the raw hearing count visible in the subhead — `5 actions · 2 matters · 8 hearing entries` —
so nothing looks dropped.

### 5.2 Group rows by matter

The four ZBA cases are not four separate stories. Cases 71/72/73 are a **variation package for one
proposed building** — lot area per unit, setbacks, parking — all heard together, all continued
together, all approved together. Case 70 is a different matter: the setback relief that let the lot
be subdivided.

Render two labelled groups:

- **Matter 1 — the subdivision** *(one lot became two)*: the 2014 map amendment + case 70-17-Z.
- **Matter 2 — the new building** *(three storeys, three units, on the other lot)*: cases
  71/72/73 + the lapse note.

Group by the project the relief serves, not by hearing date. Three variations for one building
listed flat, interleaved with an unrelated subdivision case, forces the reader to reconstruct the
grouping themselves — and most will not.

**How to group without guessing:** cases sharing a hearing date, a continuance track **and** a
common phrase in the relief description (`for a proposed three-story, three dwelling unit
building`) are one matter. Where that inference is not available, render one group of ungrouped
actions rather than inventing a relationship.

### 5.3 One row per action, and every field printed once

Map amendments, ZBA variations, special uses and administrative adjustments all render here,
newest first.

**Not a card — a row.** A card per action restated the same facts three and four times over: the
date in the card header and again in a field, "map amendment" in the title and again in a chip,
the ordinance id in the subhead and again in the docref, and a `Subject` field carrying the
address the whole report is about. Three lines carry all of it with nothing repeated:

```
[MAP AMENDMENT] [PASSED]                      Introduced & passed Jul 30, 2014
Zoning Reclassification Map No. 1-H · App No. 18063 · City Council
Ordinance O2014-4194 ↗ · Filed PDF ↗ · Chicago City Clerk (Legistar)
```

Line 1 is type, outcome, date. Line 2 is what it was. Line 3 is where to verify it. The ordinance
id moves **out** of the `WHAT CHANGED` subhead — provenance belongs with the record, not with the
finding, and it was printing twice.

### 5.4 Quote the Board where it settles a question

Case 70-17-Z's description contains the single most valuable sentence in this section:

> *"The existing two-story, two dwelling unit front building and one dwelling unit rear building
> will remain."*

Render it as a quotation on its row, attributed to the order. This is a **Board finding**, not an
applicant's description — a different and much stronger class of evidence than the 2014
application text (§4.2). Where a decision's text states a fact about the property's existing
configuration, quote it rather than paraphrasing.

---

### 5.5 Colour: a Board decision is a verdict, a map amendment is not

- Map amendment / administrative adjustment → **indigo**. A rezoning is a state. There is no good
  or bad number of them and none of them is a claim about the property's quality.
- ZBA `Approved` → **green**; ZBA `Denied` → **red**. The Board is authoritative on the exact
  question it answered, which is the standing test for red.

### 5.6 A ZBA grant shows its lapse window — the rule is 12 months

**Chicago Zoning Ordinance §17-13-1106:** an order granting a variation is valid for **12 months**
from the date of the order unless a complete building-permit application is submitted and
diligently pursued, or the use is commenced. The section states no extension provision.

So the three Jul 21, 2017 approvals here were valid to **Jul 21, 2018**. Print that on the group,
cite the ordinance section, and say what would have preserved them:

> *Each order was valid 12 months — to Jul 21, 2018 — unless a complete building-permit
> application was filed and diligently pursued, or the use commenced (§17-13-1106). Confirm with
> the Board whether these were exercised.*

⚠️ **Do not assert that they lapsed.** We know the window and we know this report shows no permits
at this address — but the building those variations serve sits on the **other** lot, and its
permits file under that address. This is §2.1's trap again, one layer down. State the window,
state that the permit count on this report does not answer it, and stop:

> *Permits for this building would sit under the other lot's address, not this one, so the permit
> count on this report does not answer it.*

An expired variation rendered with no lapse indicator is worse than not rendering it at all. A
variation asserted to have expired on evidence we do not hold is worse still.

### 5.7 Do not label an introduction date as the action date

`zoningHistory.ts:422` sets `date: introDate` — the date the matter was **introduced**, not the
date it passed. Items sort on it, and the UI prints it top-right beside a description that says
*"passed by City Council on {passedDate}"*. On this parcel the two dates coincide; in general an
ordinance is introduced months before it passes, and the row will read as though the action
happened on the filing date.

- Render **both** when they differ: `Introduced {date}` and `Passed {date}`.
- Render **one row** labelled `Introduced & passed` when they are the same day.
- Where a matter has not passed, print the status (`Introduced — referred to {committee}`) and do
  **not** describe it as an action taken.
- Sorting may stay on intro date; the label must not.

### 5.8 ZBA cases keep their applicant

`fetchAddressZba` puts the applicant into `description` with no label
(`zoningHistory.ts:479`), so it renders as an unlabelled sentence fragment. Give it a field:
`Applicant · {name}`. Keep the decision badge — `Approved` green, `Denied` red — because a Board
decision **is** authoritative on that exact question.

## 6. `WHO FILED THEM` — identical to the permit-professionals treatment

The zoning attorney is the same object as the permit professional rendered at
`RunDetail.tsx:9145–9185`. **Match that structure field for field.** That block is already the
right design and it has a slot for everything asked here:

| `.procard` / `.pro2` (permits, live) | Zoning counsel |
|---|---|
| `.prole` role label | `Zoning counsel` / `Architect` |
| `.pnm` name | `Sara K. Barnes, Esq.` |
| `.pstat` — **this property** | `Here · 1 zoning action — the 2014 map amendment that set RT-4 · passed on first reading` |
| `.pcite` — **citywide index** + `View profile →` | see §6.1 |
| `.pcite.nomatch` fallback | see §6.1 |
| `.pera` current / prior owner | `Prior owner` |

In the bold-hybrid language this is `.kyp-procard`, which already carries `.phere` (the `.pstat`
slot) and `.pcity` (the `.pcite` slot). No new component.

### 6.1 There is no citywide zoning-counsel record — so render the honest fallback

The permits block links to `/discovery?view=architect-rankings&highlight={name}` when a
professional matches the citywide index, and falls back to
*"No confident citywide match — showing this property's record only."* when they do not.

**We have no zoning-counsel index at all.** The attorney name is extracted by regex from inside a
single ordinance PDF for this one parcel; there is no citywide table to join against, and a
Legistar name search will not find her because the name is in a PDF attachment, not the matter
title. So a "view her record" link would be a fake affordance pointing at nothing.

Render `.pcity.nomatch` — *"No citywide zoning-counsel record — showing this parcel's filings
only."* — with the only real link we have: the filed PDF.

⚠️ Building the index is a **data** task, not a design one, and it is the same shape as the
county-wide tax-appeal-attorney file already on the benchmark backlog: sweep Legistar zoning
matters, extract counsel from each PDF, cache by ordinance id. Add it to that backlog. When it
lands, `.pcity` populates and the `View profile →` link turns on with **no design change** —
which is the point of matching the permits treatment now.

### 6.2 Delete the bar

The `.pwl` win/loss bar does not belong here. It encodes a **distribution** — wins against losses
across many filings — and this parcel has one filing. A bar rendered at 100% with a single segment
charts n=1, communicates nothing, and implies a success rate we did not compute. Per the standing
rule: where a claim must not be made, remove the channel rather than recolour it.

**This applies backwards to Step 13.** Gate the tax-appeal attorney's `.pwl` bar on **n ≥ 3
appeals with recorded outcomes**. Below that, print the record as text in `.phere` and draw no bar.

### 6.3 The architect renders only when named

`item.architect` is extracted by the same PDF pass (`zoningHistory.ts:135–205`) and is **not
rendered anywhere today** — a dropped field. Give it a row with role chip `Architect` **when the
filing names one.**

When it does not, **print nothing.** No placeholder, no "not named in the application" line. An
unnamed architect is not a finding — it is the ordinary case, since most map amendments have no
architect of record — and a line explaining its absence is noise that reads as a defect. This is
the same rule as the empty-state row in §1: absence of an optional attribution does not earn
space.

The one exception is §7.1's `enriched: false` — *"we did not look"* is different from *"we looked
and it was not there,"* and only the former gets a line.

### 6.4 Do not rate zoning counsel

One filing is not a track record. Use the same disclaimer register as `.profind` on the permits
block — every professional named is listed, a starting point and not a recommendation — plus the
reason they are worth showing: whoever handled the last rezoning already knows this block, this
alderman and this parcel's file, which is worth something if you plan another application.

## 7. Four data defects in `server/zoningHistory.ts`

### 7.1 Enrichment stops at three PDFs — and the UI shows a blank

`filtered.map(...)` at line 378 returns **every** matching matter, but:

- attachment URL and Councilmatic lookup run only on `top5` (line 339)
- PDF extraction stops after **three** fetches (line 360, `pdfFetches >= 3`)

So the 4th matter onward renders with `zoningAttorney`, `architect`, `fromZone` and `toZone` all
undefined — and the UI prints nothing, which reads as *"no attorney filed this one."* We did not
look. Absence of evidence is being rendered as evidence of absence.

**Fix:** carry a per-item `enriched: boolean`. Where false, render
`Attorney · not checked` in muted mono, never a blank, and state the cap in the section source
line. Best is to raise the cap and cache extractions per ordinance id — the same ordinance PDF is
re-fetched on every report run for every parcel it touches — but the display fix ships first and
independently.

### 7.2 The ZBA orders carry no attorney, and that is not the same as no attorney

`fetchAddressZba` (`zoningHistory.ts:466–486`) maps Board decisions with no PDF pass, so
`zoningAttorney` and `architect` are always undefined on ZBA items. Four of this parcel's five
actions therefore name nobody.

Do not let `WHO FILED THEM` imply that. The subhead reads `1 named · ZBA orders carry no attorney
field`, and the disclaimer says it outright: *the Board's published decisions do not carry that
field, so this is not a record of who did not appear.* Same rule as §7.1 — a field we never
collected must never render as an absence we observed.

### 7.3 The ZBA lookup defaults to ward 1

`fetchAddressZba` line 470: `const wardNum = ward ?? 1;`

When the ward does not resolve, we fetch **ward 1's** ZBA calendar and match this address against
it. It will never match, and the section will report no ZBA case with full confidence — a silent
false negative on every parcel whose ward lookup fails.

**Fix:** when `ward` is null, do not call `getZbaApprovals` at all. Return an explicit
`zbaChecked: false` and render `ZBA record not checked — ward unresolved for this address`. Never
substitute a different ward's data for a missing one.

### 7.4 The address match is a title substring, and the query is capped at 25

Line 315–316: `substringof('{num} {dir} {street}', MatterTitle)` over the 25 most recent matters.

- A rezoning filed under a co-address (`520 N Claremont` for this 520–522 parcel) is missed
  unless the title happens to contain the searched number.
- `$top=25` before keyword filtering means a busy address can silently lose older actions.

**Fix now (display):** the source line must describe what was actually searched — *"the 25 most
recent matters whose title contains this street address"* — not imply a complete history.
**Fix next (data):** search the parcel's alternate address forms, which `pinResolver` already
computes, and page past 25 when the cap is hit.

⚠️ Until 7.4 is done, **no copy anywhere in this section may claim the record is complete.**
"No rezoning on record" is accurate; "this parcel has never been rezoned" is not.

## 9. The prose budget — three blocks, not seven

A draft of this section carried **seven** blocks of explanatory text for a parcel with **one**
recorded action: a Pattern callout, four `.kyp-barkey` lines, a footnote and a source line. The
section spent more words explaining itself than reporting the record.

Most of it was **design-rule commentary leaking into product copy** — sentences describing how the
section behaves rather than what this parcel is:

- *"Map amendments, ZBA variations, special uses and administrative adjustments all render here,
  newest first"* — the reader can see what renders. Deleted; the ZBA-not-found fact moved into the
  subhead count: `1 action · no ZBA case · Chicago Councilmatic`.
- *"An applicant's description of a property is a statement of intent made to win an approval…"* —
  the column header **What it does not establish** already says this. Deleted.
- *"Both districts are read from the filed application on Chicago Councilmatic…"* — provenance,
  already in the source line. Deleted.

**Budget for this section: three prose blocks maximum.**

1. One `.kyp-barkey` under `WHO FILED THEM` — the liability disclaimer, one sentence, matching
   `.profind` on the permits block.
2. One footnote — the map-amendment-runs-with-the-land rule, stated once (§8).
3. One source line — one sentence naming the sources, no methodology essay.

**Test before adding any fourth:** would this sentence render identically on a different parcel? If
yes it is a rule, and rules go in the footnote — once. Does it describe how the section works
rather than what this property is? Then delete it; the layout is the explanation.

Subhead counters (`1 action · no ZBA case`) absorb facts that would otherwise become sentences.
Prefer them.

## 8. State the map-amendment rule in exactly one place

The distinction matters because it changes what a buyer inherits, so it earns a footnote:

> A **map amendment** permanently changes the parcel's zoning district and runs with the land — a
> new owner inherits it and it never expires. A **ZBA variation or special use is different**: it
> attaches to a specific proposal and can lapse if it is not exercised within the period the Board
> set.

**That is the only place it is defined.** The first draft of this section said the same thing
twice — once in the Pattern callout ("a map amendment is permanent, it runs with the land, it does
not expire, and it does not need to be re-earned by a new owner") and again verbatim in the
footnote, plus a third partial statement in a hero-block breakdown ("Still valid · map amendments
do not lapse"). Three statements of one rule.

The split:

- **Footnote** = the rule. What a map amendment is, and how it differs from a ZBA grant.
- **Pattern callout** = the consequence *for this parcel*, in one clause, with no re-definition:
  *"The entitlement work is done and it survives the sale, so a buyer inherits RT-4 without
  re-applying."*
- **Hero blocks** = facts only. No rule statements.

Apply the same audit to the section as a whole before shipping: **any sentence that would render
identically on a different parcel belongs in the footnote, and appears once.**
