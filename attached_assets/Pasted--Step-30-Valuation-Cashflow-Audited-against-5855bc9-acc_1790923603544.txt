## 3a. One NOI statement, for every deal

Read `val-noi-model.html` before building anything in §3b or §3d. It is the whole model on one
page, and it exists because the alternative — a different income shape per use — is how this
section became unreadable in the first place.

**Every deal is the same statement, in two halves.**

| Half | Who supplies it | Varies by deal? |
|---|---|---|
| **Property** | us — taxes from the Cook County record, insurance estimated from floor area, rent from the selected basis | no |
| **Business** | the operator — revenue, and one operating-expense figure | yes, and we have no data for it either way |

**Three rules. They are what make the double-counting question go away.**

1. **Property taxes and building insurance live in the property half only.** Taxes always from the
   county record; insurance estimated from floor area until a quote replaces it. They never appear
   in the business half.
2. **The business expense line is labelled on screen as excluding occupancy, property taxes and
   building insurance.** That is what makes a trade ratio safe to paste in — the reader knows what
   to take out of it first.
3. **Debt service is subtracted once, after NOI, never inside either half.** Owning the building
   is the mortgage; it is not an operating expense.

A corollary that settles the last ambiguity: **every square foot is in exactly one half.** Space
the business occupies produces no rent and sits in the business half as part of its cost of
operating. Space leased to someone else produces rent and sits in the property half.

### The business half is ONE line, not an itemised P&L

An earlier draft of this spec itemised six day-care expense lines — payroll, food, supplies,
program, utilities, repairs. **Drop them.** They were defaults with no source, for a cost
structure the operator knows and we do not, and they made the day care read as a different
calculator from every other deal. One entered figure with a stated exclusion carries the same
information.

The one exception is **SBA Detailed** (§3b): there the lender underwrites from SDE and the
add-backs have to be visible, so the business half gets a **Simple / Detailed** toggle. Simple —
revenue and one expense line — is the default. This is the same control the rental build-up
already has at `:18686`, in the same place; it is not a new concept.

## 3b. SBA needs its own income build-up — this does not exist today

**The gap.** The build-up at `:19194–19287` is a *rental* statement: residential rent, commercial
rent, vacancy, management, repairs, utilities, reserves. There is **no business-income model
anywhere in the section.** `rentalNoiOptions` (`:18447–18519`) contains only rent bases, so on a
business acquisition with no leased space `hasRentalOptions` is false, Step 2 never renders, and
the only route to NOI is typing one number into `valuationManualNoi`.

That is wrong for both SBA paths, and worst for business & real estate, where there are **two**
income streams and the live code models neither.

Build it as the two halves from §3a. **Simple is the default** — revenue and one expense line.
The ladder below is the **Detailed** view, for an SBA acquisition where the lender underwrites
from SDE; it is in `val-sba-income.html`.

### 20.2a — Business operating income, Detailed view

| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| Revenue | pre-fills from the listing when one is active and states it |
| Cost of goods sold · Payroll · Other operating expenses | entered |
| **Book operating profit** | subtotal |
| Add back: owner salary · owner personal expenses · one-time items | entered, tagged as add-backs |
| **Seller's discretionary earnings (SDE)** | subtotal |
| Less: market manager salary | entered — what it costs to replace the owner |
| **Business operating income (adjusted EBITDA)** | total |

SBA 7(a) underwrites from SDE **less a market manager salary**, which is why that line is
subtracted rather than left in. Add-backs are the seller's claims until a quality-of-earnings
review tests them; tag them, do not verify them.

### 20.2b — Real estate income

The existing rental statement, unchanged, covering **only space leased to someone else**. Space
the business occupies itself produces no rent.

### 20.2c — Combined

Three blocks: business operating income, real estate NOI, total. Total NOI is what 20.3 and 20.4
measure against both notes.

> **The double-count to avoid.** If the business occupies the building, its P&L already reflects
> that occupancy — it was either paying rent to the former landlord or paying none. Charging a
> market rent for the same space in 20.2b while leaving the business's earnings unadjusted counts
> the space twice. The panel must state which convention is in use. The target charges rent for
> the leased portion only and says so.

> **A combined cap rate is not a real-estate cap rate.** Dividing a blended business-plus-property
> NOI by a price that includes goodwill produces a number that looks like a cap rate and is not
> comparable to one. The 20.3 footer says so on this path.

### What the data layer needs

`listingSnapshot` (`server/listingSnapshot.ts:36–40`) extracts `rentRoll`, `grossAnnualIncome`
and `statedNoi` — all rent-oriented. A business-for-sale listing states revenue, SDE or cash flow,
and none of those have a field. **Add `revenue`, `sde` and `ebitda` to the extraction schema and
to the prompt at `:88`** so 20.2a can pre-fill the way the rental path already does. Until that
lands, only Revenue pre-fills, through `grossAnnualIncome`.

## 3d. Day Care — revenue, then an explicit NOI statement

Two changes, and the second matters more.

### The four scenarios become a 2×2, not a dropdown

`noiOptions` (`:18340–18345`) holds four: 100% efficient, 100% comfortable, 75% efficient, 75%
comfortable. Today they are a `<Select>` at `:18961–18974` — pick one, see one.

It is a **2×2**: capacity (efficient / comfortable) × enrolment (full / 75%). A dropdown is the
wrong form for a matrix the data already has. Render all four as selectable blocks, with the one
driving the metrics marked selected. See `val-dc-target.html`.

- Four `.kyp-block` elements in `.kyp-blocks.two`, laid out 2×2
- The selected one takes `aria-pressed="true"` and `.ind`; the rest take `.wash`
- `.kyp-block[aria-pressed="true"]` is **already in `kyp-base.css`** (`outline:2.5px solid
  var(--kyp-ink)`), so the selected state needs no new CSS — and a control with no visible
  selected state is broken
- Each block carries monthly net, children at that scenario, the annual figure, and the revenue
  and expense lines behind it in a `.bbreak`
- A `.kyp-bandfoot` states the range the four span and names the selected one

The range is context, not a takeaway: it says what the spread is, not whether the deal is good.

The blocks now show **revenue only**. Expenses move to their own statement — see below.

### The 75% expense ratio is replaced by an itemised NOI

This is the real fix. Today `monthlyExpense = monthlyRevenue × (expensePercent/100)` (`:18107`),
and the result is handed downstream as an NOI. **Nothing says what is inside that 75%**, and the
model behaves very differently depending on what is:

- If the ratio **excludes** property taxes and building insurance, then zeroing them downstream
  (`valuation.ts:123–124`, because `noiCoversExpenses = !isSba && hasNoi` is true on this path)
  means they are never charged at all. The deal looks better than it is.
- If the ratio **includes rent** — which a childcare operating ratio quoted in the trade usually
  does, because most operators lease — then occupancy is charged twice: once inside the ratio, and
  again as debt service. That one is a genuine double count.

Neither is visible, and no footnote resolves it.

**Replace the ratio with the two halves from §3a** — five lines total, in two blocks, with the
same **Simple / Detailed** toggle every other path has:

| Half | Line | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Revenue | the selected scenario — children × rate × 12 |
| Business | Operating expenses | entered — one line, labelled on screen **excluding occupancy, property taxes and building insurance** |
| Property | Rent from leased space | 0 — the business occupies the whole building |
| Property | Property taxes | **Cook County record** |
| Property | Building insurance | estimated from floor area |

Total NOI = business operating income + property NOI. Because the day care occupies the whole
building, its property half earns no rent and carries only the two costs of owning — which is why
property NOI is negative here, and should be.

That makes the downstream zeroing at `valuation.ts:123–124` correct *and verifiable* rather than
correct *by assumption*: taxes and insurance are visibly inside NOI, in one place, so they are
rightly not subtracted again from cash flow.

### Then 20.3 reads the result

Operating income on the Deal Inputs row **reads** the NOI from 20.2 rather than offering a second
control. `childcareViewMode`-style drift — one state written in one place, read in another — is
the bug Step 29 removed from §Project Use Analysis; do not reintroduce it here.

## 3c. Property taxes always come from our record

A standing rule, and it applies on every path:

- **Property taxes are read from the Cook County record** (`propertyTaxData`), never from the
  listing. A broker's tax figure is the seller's current bill.
- The field stays editable — the user may know something the record does not — but the tag must
  change from `From record` to `Edited` the moment it does. Today it does not (`:19231`); the
  insurance field already does this correctly (`:19030`) and is the pattern to copy.
- Cook County reassesses on transfer, so the current bill usually understates a buyer's. That
  caveat stays in the footer.
- On the **listing-stated NOI** path the taxes line is bypassed entirely, because the broker's NOI
  is "already net" (`:18530`). Show the county tax figure beside it anyway, so the reader can see
  whether the stated NOI is plausible. A listing NOI built on the seller's current bill is the
  single most common reason a stated NOI does not survive closing.

## 4. Takeaway prose — classify, don't advise

The section carries **~40 interpretive strings**, including a five-branch `verdictText` engine at
`:19306–19322`. The no-takeaways rule applies differently here than anywhere else, because the
reader supplies the inputs and 1.25× DSCR is published lender practice, not our opinion.

**Keep — these are classifications against an outside authority:**

- DSCR banded against 1.00× and 1.25× (`.kyp-bands b3`, see every target)
- The coverage rows: what the debt costs, what the lender floor needs, and the gap to each
- Honesty caveats: "estimate only, verify with a quote", "may reassess on sale", "imputed even if
  self-managed". These are **disclosure**, not interpretation. They stay.
- The formula receipts. Showing the arithmetic is not a takeaway.

**Delete — these tell the reader what to do:**

| Line | String |
|---|---|
| `:19308–19321` | the whole five-branch `verdictText`, including "expect financing pushback unless price or terms improve" and "the deal clears the bank's 1.25× minimum comfortably" |
| `:19401–19407` | the four Coverage Check paragraphs ("the deal is short at these terms", "with room to spare") |
| `:18218–18223` | the four daycare cashflow verdicts ("Cash-flow positive in every scenario", "adjust revenue or expenses above") |
| `:19363` | "a cap this high usually means an assumption is off" — keep a neutral note that cap rate above 10% is unusual, drop the diagnosis |
| `:19300–19304` | `'✓ Well-covered'`, `'! Below 1.25× lender min'`, `'✕ Doesn't cover debt'` — the band label carries this without the tick and cross |

**Replace the colour constants.** `KB = { good:'#7ee0a0', amber:'#f0c85a', bad:'#f2938c',
neu:'#9aa0cc' }` at `:19305` and the same four at `:18203–18224` go. Use `.kyp-block.grn` /
`.orange` / `.red` / `.slate`. `#f0c85a` is a tenth warm hex that exists nowhere else in the
design system.

> **Why this matters beyond house style.** "Expect financing pushback" and "the deal qualifies"
> are the outputs in this report a reader is most likely to act on. Stating the ratio against the
> published floor is a fact. Telling them what will happen to their financing is advice this
> product is not positioned to give. The 20.3 footer should say the section is not investment
> advice — it does not say so anywhere today.

## 6. The markup

### 20.1 Deal Inputs

`.kyp-calcgrid` with one `.kyp-calccol` per part of the deal. Columns that do not apply to the
selected path are **not rendered** — not hidden with CSS. Inputs are `.kyp-field` + `.kyp-input`
from patch 29; `$` and `%` affixes use `.kyp-inprefix`. Preset values use `.kyp-lock`.

### 20.2 Income → NOI

`.kyp-ledger` replaces the local `Row` helper. Three columns: label (with `<s>` sub-line),
provenance tag, value. `.kyp-lrow.sub` for subtotals, `.kyp-lrow.total` for NOI.

`Tag` becomes `.kyp-tag` with `.rec` / `.est` / `.assume`. **Provenance is context, so these drop
the verdict palette** — the live `rec` tag is green `#2f7d3f`, which reads as "good" when it only
means "sourced". Indigo for sourced, slate for assumed, dashed border for optional.

Both helpers are defined **inside the Step 2 IIFE** (`:19091`, `:19099`), so they are re-created
on every render and exist only when `showIncomeStep` is true. Lift them out or, better, delete
them in favour of the CSS.

`Row`'s `value` and `neg` props are declared but **never passed** by any of the 9 call sites —
every row supplies `edit` instead. Dead code.

### 20.3 Deal at a Glance

`.kyp-blocks.four` — cap rate and cash-on-cash in `.ind-deep`, monthly cash flow and DSCR toned by
their band. Then `.kyp-bands b3` for DSCR. The `#232d6e` band and both `.val-tile` grids go.

The ±$250 break-even window at `:19298` is **ours**, not a convention — the footer says so.

### 20.4 Coverage Check

`.kyp-cov` + `.kyp-covrow`, with `.kyp-pill good` / `att` / `bad` for the cushion. Then
`.kyp-receipt` for the arithmetic. On the 1–4 unit path (`dscrLoanApplies`, `:18636`) a second
receipt shows the DSCR-loan ratio — see `val-sf-target.html`. Keep the explanation of why the two
numbers differ; drop the four verdict paragraphs.

### 20.5 How It's Calculated

`.kyp-blocks` of `.wash` blocks plus one `.kyp-receipt`. `.val-mtiles` / `.val-mt` /
`.val-formula` go.

## 7. Persistence — the thing a user will notice first

**27 of the 28 inputs are not saved.** Only `valuationPurchasePrice` persists, through
`handleSaveAskingPrice` → `PATCH /api/runs/:id/manual-property` (`use-runs.ts:238–252`). No
localStorage, no debounced mutation, no URL state.

So a reader who sets a rate, a vacancy, a management percentage and six expense lines loses all of
it on reload, and the Save button next to the price implies the rest saved too.

This is out of scope for a restyle, and it is the largest real problem in the section. Either
persist the whole input set alongside `askingPrice`, or label the Save button so it is clear it
saves the price only. **Do not ship the restyle without doing one of the two** — the new layout
makes the inputs look more like a saved model, not less.
