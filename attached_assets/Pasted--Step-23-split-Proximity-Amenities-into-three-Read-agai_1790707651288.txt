## 0. 🔴 Ship this first, on its own — the report states something that cannot be true

`RunDetail.tsx:14708`:

```jsx
<div className="glnote"><b>No assigned high school</b> at this address — all {high.length} are
  application / lottery. Plan for the CPS choice process.</div>
```

Chicago Public Schools:

> "Every address in Chicago is assigned a neighborhood high school where students are guaranteed a
> seat without going through the GoCPS application process."
> — [cps.edu/gocps/high-school/explore/neighborhood-programs](https://www.cps.edu/gocps/high-school/explore/neighborhood-programs/)

**There is no Chicago address without an assigned high school.** So this callout is false on every
property, not just this one, and it tells a family the most alarming possible version of a situation
that does not exist.

### Why it fires

```ts
// client/src/lib/schoolsDisplay.ts:81
export function closestBoundarySchool(schools) {
  return closestSchool(schools.filter(s => s.attendanceBoundary));
}
// RunDetail.tsx:14477
const highAssigned = closestBoundarySchool(high);
```

"Assigned" is inferred as *the nearest school in the results that happens to carry an attendance
boundary*. Two faults:

1. **The search is capped at 1.5 miles** (`routes.ts:9539`, `radius = 1.5`, `Math.min(radius, 3)`).
   High-school attendance areas are much larger than elementary ones, so the assigned school is
   routinely outside the radius. It never enters the array, the filter returns nothing, and the
   callout fires.
2. **Carrying a boundary is not the same as this address being inside it.** Nothing tests the
   address against a polygon.

`school.attendanceBoundary` itself is sound — `schoolsNearby.ts:104` reads the CPS
`attendance_boundaries` field. The per-school `Boundary` / `Open Enrollment` chips are correct. Only
the leap from those to *"assigned to this address"* is unsupported.

### The fix now

Delete the `glnote` block. Replace the high-school card's assigned line with the fact that is
actually in hand:

> `4 high schools within 1.5 mi · 0 with an attendance boundary in this radius`

**Remove the word "assigned" from the high-school card entirely.** On elementary, the same inference
usually lands right because catchments are small — but it is the same unsound test, so either drop
the star there too or state the radius beside it. I would drop it; it returns properly in Step 24.

⚠ Do not "fix" this by widening the radius. A wider search finds a nearer boundary school, not the
*correct* one. The real fix is CPS attendance-boundary polygons, which is Step 24.

## 3. No takeaways — and one place colour is earned

Delete the takeaway boxes from both promoted subsections. The section one-liner states what was
found and over what radius; what it means is the user's, driven by their project use.

**Colour rule for this step:** a block takes a verdict colour **only when an outside authority has
already classified the value and set its polarity.** Otherwise it is `ind` or `dark`.

### Childcare — coloured, because the bands are INCCRRA's

Desert / underserved / adequate / plentiful are published thresholds from the Illinois Network of
Child Care Resource and Referral Agencies. "Childcare desert" is a term of art with a defined
cutoff, and INCCRRA has already decided which end is bad. Rendering *their* classification in colour
is legibility, not interpretation.

| band | children under 5 per licensed slot | block |
|---|---|---|
| plentiful | < 1.0 | **`grn`** |
| adequate | 1.0 – 1.5 | **`grn`** |
| underserved | 1.5 – 3.0 | **`orange`** |
| desert | > 3.0 | **`red`** |

The band scale below it marks the active band in the same colour. Derive the colour from the ratio
against the published thresholds — do not hardcode it per property.

### Counts — a citywide share, not colour, and not a percentile

Michelin, landmarks and galleries stay **`ind` / `dark`**. No authority has said whether a dense
restaurant cluster is good, and the polarity flips with the use: 6 Michelin restaurants within a
mile is an amenity to a residential buyer, a competitive set to a restaurateur, irrelevant to a
warehouse. That judgement is the user's.

**But a bare count is unreadable — a reader has no idea whether 6 is a lot.** Give it a denominator:

> **6** · `MICHELIN GUIDE · 1 MI` · *6 of Chicago's 105 listed restaurants*
> **0** · `JAMES BEARD · 1 MI` · *0 of 11 citywide*

Computed from `server/data/chicago_michelin_restaurants.json` (105 rows) and
`chicago_jba_restaurants.json` (11). Exact, no proxy, no banding.

⚠ **A percentile was considered and rejected.** Ranking the 1-mile count against all 77
community-area centroids gives a badly skewed scale — 57 of 77 areas have zero Michelin
restaurants, so **a single restaurant already scores the 74th percentile**, two scores 84th and
seven scores 91st. Nearly the whole range is consumed by the gap between "none" and "any," and for
James Beard, with 11 restaurants citywide, it collapses entirely. Do not add percentile banding.

⚠ **The share only works where a bounded citywide list exists.** Michelin and James Beard are
curated, published lists, so the denominator is real. **Landmarks and galleries have no usable
denominator** — `chicago_landmarks.geojson` holds 9,291 features because it includes every
contributing building in all 59 districts, not individual designations, and galleries come from
rolling business-licence records. For those two, give a qualifying fact instead of a fraction:
oldest year and notable architects for landmarks, nearest distance and source for galleries.
**Do not invent a citywide total.**
