What the Public Record Says About This Asset
Independent of what you plan to do with it

This ownership record reads like a decade of financial pressure — and the tax account is delinquent again right now.

Kalsi Sukhmit bought the property in 2013 for $475,000 with a $380,000 mortgage, then shows a state tax lien in 2017 (released the same year) and a lis pendens (foreclosure-alert filing) recorded 11/26/2019. In 2021 the owner quit-claimed the property to themself — grantor and grantee both Kalsi Sukhmit — the same day a new $712,500 mortgage was recorded, a self-transfer paired with a large refinance. A second mortgage of $198,000 followed 8/28/2025. No release is on record for the 2021 loan, so the two most recent mortgages total $910,500 ($712,500 + $198,000) in recorded debt, and the property tax account is currently marked delinquent. Whether the 2019 lis pendens was ever formally resolved isn't clear here — confirm with a full title search and counsel before offering.

The tax figure being shown is likely half the real bill — and the real bill is rising.

The evidence lists 2025 taxes at $8,944.47 ($745/month), but that's almost exactly 55% of the 2024 full-year amount ($16,262.60 × 0.55 = $8,944.43) — the standard Cook County first-installment ratio. Treat $16,262.60 as the real annual carrying cost: about $1,355/month ($16,262.60 ÷ 12), not $745. That 2024 figure is also 42% above 2023's $11,451.94 ($4,810.66 higher). Payment status is listed as delinquent — confirm the exact amount owed and any redemption deadline with the Treasurer before closing.

There's more unused building capacity here than exists in the building itself.

RT-4 zoning caps this 2,967 SF lot at 1.2 FAR, or 3,560 SF of buildable area (2,967 × 1.2). The existing 1,638 SF building sits at 0.55 FAR, leaving 1,922 SF of remaining capacity — more square footage than the current structure. RT-4 allows up to 3 units per standard lot as-of-right, and the funnel flags no zoning-relief requirement, consistent with an as-of-right addition or multi-unit conversion. The parcel also sits in a federally designated Opportunity Zone. Height is capped at 38 ft / 3 stories.

There's no recent arm's-length sale to benchmark value against.

The last true purchase on record is the 2013 Trustees Deed at $475,000. Every transfer since — the 2021 quit-claim deed — moved title from Kalsi Sukhmit to Kalsi Sukhmit with no sale amount, not to a new buyer. Any valuation here has to be built off the 2013 basis, not a fresh market transaction.

Transit access here is genuinely strong, independent of what gets built.

The Western Ave Metra station (Milwaukee North/West, North Central Service) is 0.19 miles away and qualifies the site as TOD-eligible. CTA bus routes 65 (Grand) and 49/X49 (Western) stop roughly 0.06–0.07 miles out, and the Damen Green Line station is 0.62 miles away. Real support for future residential demand once the title and tax issues are cleared — but it doesn't offset the risk items above.

Full build-out to 3 units still has to clear a parking test on a small lot.

RT-4 requires 1 parking space per unit. Fitting 3 on-site spaces on a 2,967 SF lot alongside any addition is a real physical constraint, not just paperwork — confirm a workable site plan with an architect before underwriting the full 1,922 SF of remaining FAR as usable.
