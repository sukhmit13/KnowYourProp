The preparatory news pass froze article selection and the publisher-based split while correcting unit evidence and local logos. That freeze was local to that pass, not a standing restriction: a later request widened Neighborhood News to a rolling year without shortening the longer property-specific search. While the legacy Corridor Intelligence section exists, it must not repeat articles visible in News.

**Why:** The authoritative scope boundary explicitly froze which articles appear and how they split, even though its source-pooling and keyword-removal checklist would change that set. The top-level boundary takes precedence over those conflicting checklist items.

**How to apply:** Do not undo the newer, distinct window lengths or merge Corridor Intelligence into News merely to prevent duplication. When a later behavior pass changes article selection, pool available feeds, replace the publisher filter with subject classification, remove restaurant/hotel/retail from development filtering, and review window-specific retrieval together. Then retire the preparatory freeze.

The supplied Step 22 continuation calls for classifying by subject first, adding Development & Real Estate without deleting legacy content, changing News to News & Culture, and only then deleting the legacy Corridor Intelligence rendering while retaining server-side corridor detection for scope chips and on-corridor captions. Do not restyle the legacy section; the attached continuation explicitly supersedes that approach.

**Why:** Restyling the legacy section would invest in a layout slated for deletion and preserve the publisher/geography split that causes duplicate stories.

**How to apply:** Keep those migration steps separate, verify article classification before changing sections, and treat deletion as a distinct destructive step after the new sections have been checked against real reports.

The rolling-year Neighborhood News archive can retain only stories discovered while they are still available in a source feed. It cannot reconstruct stories already rotated out before collection began.

**Why:** RSS feeds and search feeds expose limited, changing windows; a 365-day date filter by itself does not guarantee 365 days of historical discovery.

**How to apply:** Preserve discovered articles in durable storage rather than relying on an in-memory feed cache or the current top-results cap. Describe the first year's archive as accumulating coverage, not a complete historical backfill.
